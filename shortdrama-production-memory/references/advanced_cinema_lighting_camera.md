# Advanced Cinema Lighting And Camera

Use this reference when the user asks for top-tier cinematic image prompts, storyboards, or Seedance 2.0 video prompts. Keep every rule executable: write what the camera sees, where light comes from, how subjects move, and where the edit lands.

## Lighting Standard

- Light must serve story relation first: main light reveals the subject, negative fill shapes the face, side/back light separates the body from the background, and background light creates depth.
- Never use flat light. Define light direction, light ratio, shadow density, edge separation, and whether dark areas retain texture.
- For faces, specify which side receives key light, which side falls into shadow, whether the eyes catch light, and how the jaw, cheekbone, nose bridge, and costume edge are separated.
- For night scenes, do not simply darken the image. Use motivated sources such as fire, moonlight, window light, lanterns, magic glow, vehicle light, or reflected ground light.
- For fantasy effects, every spell or energy source must produce physical lighting: face color cast, rim glow, ground reflection, smoke translucency, and shadow movement.

## Lens And Composition

- Choose focal length for psychological effect: wide lens for space pressure and speed, 35mm for human-environment balance, 50mm for direct observation, 85mm+ for compression, isolation, and gaze tension.
- Composition must express power: low angle for threat or hero weight, high angle for vulnerability or tactical overview, eye-level for confrontation or equal dialogue.
- Use foreground only in medium or close shots, and only with valid scene elements. Foreground stays blurred and never steals the subject.
- Characters, props, troops, and environmental objects must be staggered. Avoid neat rows unless the script explicitly requires a formal formation.
- The main face must be clear; the subject edge must be sharp and visibly separated from the background.

## Camera Movement

- Movement starts only when information changes. Push in for realization, pull out for revealed consequence, track for pursuit or pressure, orbit for relationship shift, whip pan for sudden transfer of attention.
- Every moving shot must define start point, path, speed, focus target, subject action, and landing point.
- For action scenes, cut on contact or force transfer: blade collision, spell impact, body recoil, foot landing, cloth snap, dust burst, or spark explosion.
- For dialogue scenes, maintain axis and eyeline. Use listener reactions when emotional absorption is more important than the speaker's face.

## Production Prompt Use

- 融图: write dominant color, auxiliary colors, main/fill/back/rim light, material texture, subject edge separation, face clarity, and exact body posture.
- Seedance: write consecutive motion blocks with transition method, shot size, light change, camera path, focus shift, subject action, dialogue, sound, and cut point.
- Storyboard: draw or describe camera cone, role positions, movement arrows, light direction, foreground/background layers, and the next-shot bridge.

## Deakins-Style Low-Key Lighting

- Start from the motivated source: window, door crack, practical lamp, moon, fire, vehicle, screen, spell, sword energy, or formation glow.
- Keep the dark side shaped by negative fill. Do not let ambient light flatten the face.
- For a face, define the lit side, the shadow side, eye catchlight, cheekbone, nose bridge, jaw line, and costume edge separation.
- Background lights should add depth and readable texture, not compete with the character.
- In fantasy action, spell effects must act as real light sources: color cast on skin, rim light on clothing, reflections on weapons and ground, and translucent smoke edges.

## Cinematic Night Protocol

- Night scenes need designed contrast, not just low brightness.
- Use cool exterior tones against warm interior or fire/practical tones when the scene supports it.
- Keep large dark areas, but preserve at least one readable texture: ground reflection, smoke, wall pattern, costume edge, weapon edge, or silhouette.
- Night action is read through side light, back light, rim glow, dust, sparks, and body feedback, not front fill.

## Lens And Emotional Translation

- 24/28mm: space pressure, chase speed, dive motion, battlefield scale.
- 35mm: human-environment balance, medium-shot blocking, group action.
- 50mm: direct observation, tense gaze, confrontation before impact.
- 85mm+: eye tension, listener reaction, compression, isolation.
- Do not name lenses as decoration. Always state the visible result: compression, speed, isolation, pressure, clarity, or spatial depth.

## Director-Led Montage Rules

- Every shot answers four questions: what does the viewer learn, what do they feel, where should they look, and what does the next shot receive?
- Dialogue scenes are not alternating face coverage. Use speaker close-ups for key lines and listener close-ups when reaction carries the emotion.
- Long dialogue must be broken with reaction, prop, hand, environment, or spatial consequence shots.
- Farewell and loss scenes need process: route, pause, object, back view, empty space, sound fading, and the person left behind.
- AI video prompts must define director choices: viewpoint, subject distance, relationship distance, axis, screen direction, motion path, focus transfer, and edit landing point.

## Scale Reveal And Action Entrance

- Do not reveal a giant monster, mech, magic formation, or demon general at full scale immediately.
- Build the reveal: omen -> physical detail -> human scale reference -> reaction -> low-angle or wide full reveal -> environmental consequence.
- Useful details: cracking ground, falling dust, weapon dragging sparks, footstep imprint, lights shaking, wind pressure lifting costume edges, smoke parting.
- Cut action on physical transfer: blade contact, shield hit, foot landing, spell impact, recoil, cloth snap, dust burst, spark explosion.

## Qualified Mecha Texture Standard

Use this standard when the user asks for Pacific-rim-scale mecha, heavy industrial robots, or rainy night giant-machine images. The target is live-action cinematic realism, not toy-like CG, cartoon armor, or flat concept art.

- Shape language: broad armored torso, thick shoulders, heavy forearms, compact armored head, visible mechanical neck, layered chest plates, large fist or weapon silhouette, and asymmetry in shoulder/arm equipment so the design does not feel generic.
- Helmet close-up language: armored pilot-helmet head, narrow yellow or amber visor, recessed black faceplate, cheek vents, jaw guard, small red sensor lights, heavy collar armor, and sloped shoulder plates that frame the head.
- Armor material: dark gunmetal, blue-gray steel, titanium plates, ceramic-black panels, beveled edges, scratches, chipped paint, soot, rain streaks, wet specular highlights, oil marks, and panel seams.
- Mechanical structure: hydraulic pistons, hinge rings, rotating joints, exposed cabling only in protected recesses, vents, bolts, heat sinks, actuator rods, nested finger joints, and layered elbow/knee mechanisms. Keep the logic readable; avoid random noise details.
- Rain-night finish: heavy rain falling across the lens, water running over curved armor, droplets collecting on shoulder domes, mist around joints, blue-white rim light cutting the body edge, and small red/blue signal lights embedded in the chest or head.
- Rain-fog hero close-up finish: cold blue-gray atmosphere, thick mist crossing the armor, soft sky backlight, wet curved metal catching long vertical highlights, yellow visor glowing through rain, and red accent stripes or warning lights used sparingly.
- Scale cues: low-angle camera, partial body cropping, searchlights, buildings, cranes, vehicles, rain shafts, foreground platform edges, or human-scale elements near the feet.
- Close-up scale cues: crop the shoulders and head so the body exceeds the frame, let background structures or distant drones appear tiny in fog, and use drifting rain/mist layers to show depth.
- Lighting: cold blue night base, strong back/rim light, bright wet-metal highlights, red warning lights, blue signal lights, and occasional orange fire reflection. Never use flat light.
- Prompt words to include when no design sheet exists: `原创巨型重装机甲`, `厚重弧面装甲`, `深灰钛合金湿润金属`, `黄色窄 visor 面罩`, `红色警示灯组`, `蓝白冷色轮廓光`, `雨水沿装甲接缝流淌`, `雨雾包裹肩甲和头盔`, `液压关节和机械指节清晰`, `真实高预算科幻电影实拍质感`.
- Negative rules: no plastic toy feel, no rubber suit feel, no flat gray blocks, no neat single-piece armor, no random greeble noise, no bright clean showroom metal, no 2D illustration, no anime rendering, no game screenshot look.

## Opening Hook And Gap

- A violent opening is not defined by gore. It is a sudden gap between expected direction and the direction the character is forced into.
- Establish normal order first: place, character relation, routine action, and screen direction must be readable within the first shot group.
- Break that order with one visible abnormal detail: sound source, shadow, object movement, impossible light, threat entry, ground vibration, or changed reaction from a crowd.
- Give immediate body feedback: held breath, frozen fingers, tightened shoulders, eye shift, half-step back, cloth lifted by pressure, or weapon grip tightening.
- Land the first scene on a question the viewer wants answered: what changed, who arrived, why danger is near, or what action the character must now take.

## Image2 Clean Photoreal Anti-Speckle Rule

Use this when Image2 creates star-like speckles, grain, noisy water, broken lines, noisy monster skin, or painterly fragments.

- Prefer `modern digital cinema photography`, `clean sharp image`, `continuous subject edges`, `smooth unbroken metal lines`, `large continuous highlights`, and `defocused background shapes`.
- Do not use `Cinematic Grain`, film grain, natural lens defects, 35mm grain, dense rain droplets, splash spray, debris, sparks, far window lights, small fire points, dense scales, rough granular skin, or very fine scratches in complex rain-night scenes.
- Background must be intentionally simplified: distant city, towers, bridges, cars, smoke, and streets become soft dark defocused shapes with no small lights or hard detail.
- Rain should be a stable background layer: thin transparent streaks mainly in dark background areas, not white dots on the subject.
- Ground water should be black mirror wet asphalt, continuous water film, broad horizontal ripples, and low smooth waves. Avoid spray, foam, mud dots, gravel, floating debris, and particle-like splash.
- Monster surface should use large continuous wet keratin plates, broad skin folds, continuous bone spines, and broad sheet-like wet highlights. Avoid dense small scales and granular texture.
- Mecha wear should use large plates, continuous brushed metal, a few long scratches, edge wear, oil film, and continuous rim highlights. Avoid random greeble noise, dense scratches, broken silhouettes, and spotty highlights.
- Key prompt sentence: `The image must be clean, sharp, and smooth; subject contours are continuous and complete, metal lines are unbroken, surface highlights are broad and continuous, with no star-like speckles, no grain, no broken edges, no noisy rain, no particle splash, and no painterly texture.`
